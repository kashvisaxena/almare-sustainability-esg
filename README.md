Almare — Sustainability, Stakeholder & ESG Strategy

Project Overview

This project develops a sustainability strategy for Almare, a seaweed-based biodegradable packaging concept designed as an alternative to conventional single-use plastic.

The work combines ESG thinking, stakeholder analysis, materiality assessment, circular-economy principles and measurable environmental targets within an ESRS-aligned reporting approach.

Project Objective

The objective was to show how a sustainable packaging concept can move beyond a product idea and be structured around measurable environmental, social and governance priorities.

Analysis Scope

The project focused on:

- Seaweed-based biodegradable packaging
- Sustainability reporting
- ESG framework
- European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)
- Stakeholder analysis
- Double materiality
- Materiality matrix
- Circular-economy strategy
- Environmental targets
- B2B partnerships
- Performance objectives and KPIs

Sustainability Framework

The project organised the strategy around three ESG dimensions:

Environmental
Reducing plastic waste, supporting biodegradable packaging and lowering environmental impact.

Social
Considering employees, suppliers, coastal communities, customers and business partnerships.

Governance
Supporting transparency, ethics and structured sustainability reporting.

Stakeholder Analysis

The project identified key stakeholder groups including:

- B2B customers and end consumers
- Investors
- Seaweed and material suppliers
- Government and policy makers
- Local communities

The analysis considered how sustainability decisions could affect these groups and how stakeholder expectations could influence the business.

Double Materiality

The project used a double-materiality perspective to consider both:

- the environmental and social impact of the business; and
- the financial and business impact of sustainability-related issues.

The materiality analysis highlighted plastic pollution, ocean health, sustainable sourcing, climate impact, cost efficiency and sustainable-material innovation as important topics.

Strategic Priorities

The proposed strategy was built around:

- Continuous product and material innovation
- Partnerships with local seaweed farms
- Circular-economy principles
- Sustainable B2B growth
- Gradual market expansion
- ESG and ESRS-aligned reporting

Performance Targets

The academic project proposed measurable targets including:

- replacing approximately 100 tonnes of conventional plastic annually;
- working toward a 30% CO₂-emission reduction over five years;
- building approximately 40–50 B2B partnerships over five years; and
- improving biodegradability and production-cost efficiency.

These are project targets created for academic analysis and should not be interpreted as actual company commitments or audited ESG results.

Overall Conclusion

The project demonstrates how sustainability can be converted into a structured business strategy through stakeholder analysis, materiality assessment, measurable targets and performance indicators.

It also shows the role of ESG and ESRS-style reporting in connecting environmental impact with business decision-making, transparency and long-term growth.
